## Quick recap

The team met to discuss progress on their tensegrity robot project and address recent challenges with the competition's budget requirements and rules. Hasith shared concerns about the competition organizers' last-minute changes to the budget rules, including penalties for being over or under budget by $50, which he felt unfairly punished their well-prepared BOM submission. The team reviewed their modified BOM that included cost-saving substitutions for components while maintaining functionality, with Hasith explaining his decisions to use Amazon alternatives despite lower specifications. David reported working on SOLIDWORKS designs and planning to create a smaller test model for 3D printing using his personal printer, while Justin mentioned ongoing work on a ratcheting mechanism for string tension simulation. The team discussed their frustration with the competition's presentation requirements being imposed at the last minute and shared concerns about other teams' incomplete BOMs. Hasith demonstrated GitHub project management tools and offered to teach the team members coding skills for the microcontroller, with Justin expressing interest in participating in the coding work. The conversation ended with Hasith planning to send a follow-up email to the competition organizers about the delayed parts ordering process.

## Summary

### Project Status and Next Steps

The team discussed project status and next steps. David reported he wants to continue the project and is looking for SOLIDWORKS software to design a smaller model that he hopes to print using his bamboo printer, though he may need to visit a relative's place to access it. Hasith offered to help by printing the model on campus using the library printers if David needs assistance. The discussion ended with Hasith preparing to demonstrate how to effectively use and set up Git for documentation purposes.

### GitHub Model Development Discussion

Hasith discussed adding models to GitHub and requested Justin to build a ratcheting mechanism for simulating motor functionality in their project. He mentioned that Justin was working on dimensions for the model but had not yet resolved the issues. Jaylin expressed concerns about last-minute rule changes but confirmed his willingness to continue with the project, asking if a full assembly in SolidWorks had been completed.

### CAD Model Access and Sharing

The team discussed CAD model access and file sharing through Teams, with David explaining that Yale GrabCAD models are available in the CAD folder and the tri-bar file contains the assembled robot design. Hasith and David clarified that accessing SolidWorks requires using Citrix due to their Mac computers. The group also discussed a recent email response regarding design considerations and time constraints, with Justin noting they should continue working on CAD models while waiting for electronics components including PCBs and motors. Hasith mentioned reviewing and updating the bill of materials, which had been significantly modified, and planned to explain the reasoning behind the changes.

### BOM Component Cost Optimization Discussion

Hasith presented changes to the original BOM, including replacing expensive components with more affordable alternatives from Amazon. He highlighted that while cheaper options could reduce costs significantly (such as motors dropping from $33 to $6 per unit), these alternatives lacked proper specifications and ratings. Hasith expressed concerns about cutting corners by using unspecified components, noting that Yale had not taken this approach with their original specifications.

### Competition Budget and Penalties Discussion

David and Hasith discussed the competition budget constraints, with David expressing willingness to exceed the $750 limit if necessary. Hasith shared concerns about new penalty rules that would deduct 50 points for every $50 deviation from the targeted budget, which could significantly impact their scoring even if they complete the required robot functionality. They noted that building a complete untethered robot based on the Yale design could potentially earn them around 40 points for basic movement and directed movements, though autonomous functionality would be more challenging to implement.

### Competition Budget Spending Limit Issues

Hasith and David discussed issues with the competition budget, specifically regarding conflicting information about the allowed spending limit. Hasith expressed frustration that while they were told to keep under $500, they were privately informed they could go up to $750 due to fewer teams joining the competition. David noted that Yale's reference design from 2020 cost around $800 without accounting for inflation and required discounts to reach that price point, suggesting current component costs would be higher.

### 3D Component Design Modifications

Hasith and David discussed design modifications for a 3D printed component, focusing on creating visibility for LEDs on the PCB and removing unnecessary connectors. David confirmed it would be possible to add a slot to make the LEDs visible and suggested they could potentially redesign other mechanical components if time permits. They also briefly mentioned a 10-week timeline for the project, though David noted that progress might be delayed due to BOM issues.

### Project Design and Component Updates

The team discussed printing options for a clear resin piece and the mechanical design of their project, including the use of aluminum rods and clamping mechanisms. Hasith expressed concerns about budget constraints and the potential for other teams to use additional components from existing projects, while David focused on getting the best electronics components possible for learning purposes. The discussion revealed that their BOM updates caused a delay in ordering components, moving the timeline from Monday/Tuesday delivery to a later Tuesday deadline, though they noted that other teams' initial BOMs were also problematic and would likely need updates.

### Robot Competition Format Issues

The team discussed issues with the robot competition format, particularly regarding last-minute presentation requests and sharing of components between teams. Hasith expressed frustration about the inconsistent approach, noting that the organizers were sharing components between groups while also trying to treat it as a formal competition with presentations and rubrics. The team agreed they should have shared components earlier, though there was some confusion about who was present during the relevant discussions.

### Competition Presentation Issues Discussion

Hasith and David discussed their negative experience presenting at a competition where they encountered issues with another team member who claimed ownership of their design and tried to recruit Hasith away from their team. They presented their slides comparing designs, including PCB designs and battery systems, and mentioned that PCBs had already been ordered in bulk. Both expressed disappointment with the organization's handling of the competition, particularly noting poor communication and lack of response to their setup requests.

### Robot Competition Strategy Planning

Hasith and David discussed their team's approach to the competition, agreeing that having a working robot would give them an advantage even if they face budget constraints with the 700 cap. David suggested they might be the only team with a functional robot by the end of the semester, which would help them win regardless of points lost. The team agreed to focus on mechanical design work using SOLIDWORKS, with Hasith recommending against using GitHub for CAD files and suggesting they upload the final project to their ZV linked profile instead. Hasith also noted that project management and documentation have been added to the rubric, highlighting their team's strong documentation practices.

### GitHub Documentation and Project Management

Hasith demonstrated GitHub documentation practices and project management features to David, showing how README files and issues can be used to organize project content and track progress. The team discussed following up on a BOM (Bill of Materials) that was sent earlier that morning, with Hasith planning to send a follow-up email on Monday to get an update on the status since the delay could impact their end-of-semester timeline.

### Microcontroller Programming Team Discussion

Hasith discussed the technical aspects of microcontroller programming with the team, explaining the basic architecture including CPU, memory, and GPIO ports. He outlined two options for implementing the project: either he could complete it independently within 8 hours, or the team could learn the process through hands-on experience using GitHub for collaboration and code reviews. Hasith asked the team members about their interest in learning the coding aspects, noting that Jaylin had previous experience with microcontrollers while David's experience was limited to a freshman lab.

### Microcontroller Project Planning Discussion

Hasith discussed a microcontroller project involving a Nano 33 board with Wi-Fi capabilities for motor control, though he noted he hasn't worked with this specific hardware before. David expressed interest but raised concerns about time availability due to five courses with projects due in November, while Justin volunteered to help with coding. Hasith shared his calendar and suggested team members add their availability constraints to a shared Google sheet, noting he would be out of town from November 1-7 for a conference.
